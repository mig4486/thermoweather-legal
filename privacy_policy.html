ThermoWeather Privacy Policy

Effective 12 October 2026 · Developer: tgstartup, contact — tgstartup@icloud.com

Last updated 13 November 2026

Version scope. This notice describes ThermoWeather 1.1.0 (build 34), including its Sky section, local acoustic sessions, camera and event catalogs, and optional subscription features. Earlier installations may use different defaults. In particular, versions 1.0.17–1.0.19 offered opt-in usage analytics and crash reports; new installations of 1.1.0 use the defaults described in section 3. Saved choices are preserved on update. Availability depends on the build, device, permissions, region, source coverage and subscription. Updating this notice does not update the installed app.

ThermoWeather (the “app”, “we”) shows weather forecasts and brings together temperature values from external BLE sensors and manual entry. The iPhone itself does not measure room-air or body temperature in degrees, and the app is not a medical device. The app is designed to collect as little as possible and to process measurements on your device unless you deliberately export a supported result.

1. Measurements and processing on your device

Microphone recordings and acoustic sessions. The microphone is accessed after permission and an explicit measurement start. In 1.1.0 the main acoustic entry plays a short probe through the built-in speaker and records it through supported microphones, without requiring a wall. The direct measurement flow requires confirmation of local audio recording. WAV recordings, probe audio and session records can remain in the app’s local storage, including incomplete attempts. They can contain nearby speech or other background sounds as well as the probe. Session records may contain device and audio-route details, timestamps, preparation choices, geometry/calibration information and reference temperature/humidity readings, including a local reference-sensor identifier where supplied. Analysis runs on the device. These files are not automatically uploaded to the developer or to analytics, advertising or subscription services. A separate legacy echo method processes short samples without retaining them as these saved sessions. Do not record conversations or other people without appropriate permission.

Manual body-temperature values. If you enter body temperature manually, the value is handled only in current-session memory. It is not written to the app’s local database or app-container backup and is never sent to our servers, Firebase Analytics, Google AdMob, Adapty, or any other third party.

Local ambient-temperature log. External Bluetooth sensor readings and manual room-air entries are stored locally, together with supported sensor names, notes and measurement metadata. Saved acoustic results may include quality, calibration and reference information. Humidity comes from the indoor source or explicit input identified by the feature, or a disclosed assumption; outdoor humidity is not silently substituted for indoor humidity. Manual body-temperature values remain session-only. We do not operate a server that receives your measurement history automatically.

Recommendations and lunar calendar. Calculations for comfort, moisture, ventilation and drying combine available local readings and forecast data on the device. Room values are not added to weather-provider requests. Lunar calculations, selected dates and reminder settings are handled locally; weather for the selected place is requested separately where needed.

Best Time widget (1.0.19). To show times for airing a room, drying laundry or observing the Moon, the app saves a display summary in a local container shared with its iOS widget. It can include the place name, time window, status, reason, language and update time. A local token opens the matching view in the app. The widget reads this file on your device. The shared file omits exact coordinates, raw indoor readings and sensor identifiers. The app refreshes or clears the summary as its data changes. Anyone who can see the widget may see the place and suggested time. You can remove it from your Home Screen.

Airing timer (1.0.19). When you start a timer, the app gives iOS its start and end times, a per-timer identifier, display language and, when available, the room label. iOS can show the countdown and room label on the Lock Screen or Dynamic Island. The app updates and ends the activity locally. It does not register the timer for remote push updates. You can stop the timer in the app or manage Live Activities in iOS settings.

Spoken summaries and compass. When you tap a speaker button, the app passes a summary of the displayed weather, thermometer or Sky information to the operating system’s text-to-speech engine. It does not record your voice or send it to a developer-operated speech service. System voices, downloads and processing follow operating-system settings and terms. The Moon/Sun orientation view uses available device heading/motion and the selected place locally; location permission alone does not provide a compass sensor. Speech does not start automatically.

Sharing a result. In builds with sharing, you can preview and export a supported saved indoor reading, a daily forecast, or a drying/ventilation window as an image or copy its text. In 1.0.19 you can also share a Moon observation window. Its preview can show the place name, local date, time range, time zone, forecast cloud cover, lunar illumination and forecast update time. A card may contain the selected city's name, date and time, temperature, humidity, units, source and recommendation. Sensor names, notes, record identifiers, exact coordinates and body-temperature values are excluded from these card templates. Creating a preview does not upload it to us. Opening the system share menu passes the selected card and text to the operating system and, if you choose one, a receiving app or service; copying puts text on the system clipboard. Your chosen recipient, service, clipboard settings or account synchronization may store or send that content under their own terms. Review the preview before sharing. We do not receive the recipient or a delivery confirmation.

Notifications. With notification permission, the app schedules local reminders for supported ventilation/drying plans, Moon phases and observation windows, and aurora conditions. Settings can retain the chosen place and its coordinates, time zone, thresholds and local schedule. Aurora reminders are prepared from fresh Kp and weather forecasts while the app can refresh them; they are not continuous remote detection or server push alerts. Sensor threshold alerts use the selected sensor and available fresh readings. The operating system receives notification content, times and navigation identifiers; a room name or reading may appear on the lock screen. System restrictions, stale data, disconnected sensors or revoked permission may prevent checks or delivery. Optional return-offer reminders have their own in-app choice. No email or phone number is required for these local notifications.

2. Data shared with services

Location. With your permission, coordinates (coarsened to forecast-level precision) are sent to WeatherAPI.com to fetch your local forecast. We keep no movement history. You may pick a city manually instead, in which case no location access is needed.

Forecast and weather map. WeatherAPI.com receives the forecast coordinates or selected city, weather request parameters and IP address. For weather map layers (such as clouds, precipitation, temperature, wind and pressure), WeatherAPI Maps receives tile indices, zoom level, selected layer and forecast hour, IP address and technical request headers. Tile indices identify the area being viewed. See the WeatherAPI.com Privacy Policy.

Geomagnetic activity. When you open the geomagnetic activity screen, the app requests the global planetary Kp index from NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center. Requests disclose your IP address and technical request headers to NOAA; they do not include your location, sensor readings or room measurements. The app caches the response locally. See the NOAA Privacy Policy.

Aurora, history and seasons. NOAA SWPC supplies global space-weather and aurora-model data. Global requests disclose IP addresses and technical headers, but do not send your room readings or selected coordinates to NOAA for the global model. Supported alternative builds may also use MapMap global metadata and imagery. Historical Kp requests to GFZ contain the requested dates/index, not your location. Bundled ERA5 cloud climatology, GFZ seasonal summaries and curated historical-observation data are used locally where included. Matching these records to the selected place is local; separate weather/history requests to WeatherAPI include the place and requested dates. The app can retain forecast snapshots and source responses locally. Opening a source link connects to that source. See NOAA privacy information, GFZ data protection, and MapMap privacy information.

Live cameras and panoramas. Choosing a camera connects to its operator and the original media player, through an embedded player, an in-app WebView or a periodically refreshed image. Operators in the current catalog include FMI, University of Calgary/AuroraMAX, IRF, Starlapland, Lights Over Lapland, Nature of Jokkmokk, UNIS/KHO, Visit Lyngenfjord, NAOJ/Subaru, CFHT, NOAA, NASA, NPS/Explore and Roundshot/Pursuit. YouTube and Twitch can provide the embedded player. The operator/player receives your IP address, requested page or video/camera identifier, device/browser metadata and playback interactions; their cookies, local storage, diagnostics and advertising practices may apply. The app may check the official page to resolve the current player when opening it. We do not rehost the stream and do not send indoor readings or location coordinates in these camera requests. Viewing a remote camera does not activate your own camera or microphone. Media viewing may keep the screen awake and use substantial mobile data. Catalog updates can add or remove supported sources; each camera identifies its source. See Google/YouTube privacy, Twitch privacy, and the selected operator’s policy. Older road-camera features use Fintraffic Digitraffic and disclose the selected camera/preset and technical request data.

Astronomical events and offline catalogs. The app includes a translated event catalog prepared from NASA, IMO and USNO sources and a built-in camera directory. Valid updates received through Firebase Remote Config replace or supplement the supported catalog and are saved locally for later offline access. Event text is supplied as translated catalog content; the app does not send your selected dates, personal text or measurements to a translation service. Reading a cached event does not request its source website; opening its source or broadcast does. Offline catalog availability does not make live video or fresh forecasts available without a connection.

U.S. official warnings. When the selected place is in the United States or a U.S. territory, the app requests active warnings for that place from the U.S. National Weather Service (api.weather.gov, part of NOAA). The request contains the place's coordinates rounded to 0.01° (about 1 km, because warnings are issued per county or zone), your IP address and technical request headers including the app's contact address; it does not include sensor readings or room measurements. Places outside the U.S. are never sent. The app caches the response locally. See the NOAA Privacy Policy.

Regional cyclone and tsunami bulletins. When you open these source screens or select the corresponding event layer on the map, the app requests the current tropical cyclone summary from NOAA NHC/CPHC and public CAP bulletins from NOAA tsunami warning centers (NTWC or PTWC). The warnings map checks both centers. These requests disclose your IP address and technical request headers, but do not include your location, sensor readings or room measurements. The cyclone map also downloads the official forecast cone and track files (KMZ/KML) from www.nhc.noaa.gov, using links in the cyclone summary. These requests likewise disclose IP addresses and technical headers, but do not contain the selected place's coordinates or indoor measurements. Responses and forecast graphics are cached locally. The sources have regional coverage; their records are not a determination of danger at your selected place. Opening an official bulletin link also makes a request to that source. See the NOAA Privacy Policy.

Earthquakes. When you open the Observations tab of the hazards screen or select the earthquake layer on the map, the app requests recent earthquakes near the selected place from the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS). The request contains that place's coordinates rounded to 0.1° (about 11 km), plus your IP address and technical request headers; it does not include sensor readings or room measurements. While the earthquake layer is shown, the app also requests earthquakes in the part of the map you are viewing; that request contains the edges of the visible area rounded outward to a grid of 1° to 15°, not your location. The app stores the response for the selected place on the device; responses for map areas are kept only in memory while the app is running. See the USGS privacy policy.

Base map. When you open or pan the map, the app requests images directly from OpenStreetMap Foundation servers. These requests disclose the IP address, tile indices and zoom level (the viewed area), and technical headers including the app name. They do not contain sensor readings or your temperature log. Denying location access does not hide the IP address or viewed area from map providers. See the OpenStreetMap Foundation Privacy Policy.

Map cache. Viewed tiles are stored in the device's cache directory to reduce repeated network requests. The operating system may clear this cache; it is removed when the app is deleted. We do not receive a copy of it.

3. Analytics and advertising

Product analytics. On a new installation of 1.1.0, “Usage analytics” is enabled from the first application screen without a separate onboarding prompt. You can turn it off in Settings → Privacy → Your privacy choices. Saved choices are preserved when updating, including a previous refusal; an existing completed installation without a valid saved choice does not receive a new default grant. While enabled, Google Analytics for Firebase receives app interactions, sessions, app-instance identifiers, app/OS/device information and bounded feature/subscription-funnel events. Analytics can derive approximate location from network information; our weather events may contain a country, but do not include forecast coordinates. Reports can be aggregated while the underlying events remain pseudonymous rather than anonymous. Our custom-event filter excludes temperature values, body categories, precise coordinates, notes, sensor names, spoken text and audio.

With usage analytics enabled, subscription-funnel events also include the observed onboarding variant, its first observed light/dark appearance, the current appearance, and the subscription-screen trigger and outcome. Events for features such as sharing, speech and measurement attempts record bounded action/state categories, not the result content, city name, spoken text, audio or recipient. We store this bounded origin on the device to keep it consistent across app restarts and purchase results. It is cleared when usage analytics is disabled. Events before collection begins are not reconstructed, and restoring an existing subscription is distinguished from a new purchase. These fields contain no new user or transaction identifier.

In 1.0.19, optional usage analytics can also report an installation environment such as TestFlight, production or unknown. Moon observation events can report the action taken, a day or night period, a cloud or light category, and a reminder or viewing-window outcome. These events exclude the place, exact observation time and coordinates. Acoustic feature events can include the setup scenario, calibration status and a result or failure category. They exclude audio, room dimensions and calibration values.

Crash diagnostics. On new installations of 1.1.0, “Crash reports” is also enabled without a separate onboarding prompt. It has a separate Settings switch and saved choices are preserved on update. While enabled, Firebase Crashlytics receives crash and handled-error reports, stack traces, app/OS/device details and installation identifiers. Custom fields are limited to technical states; we do not intentionally attach readings, coordinates, sensor names, notes, audio, email addresses or advertising identifiers. Handled-error text is sanitized; uncaught crashes can still contain runtime error details.

Configuration, catalogs and experiments. Firebase Remote Config uses installation identifiers and technical information to deliver operational controls, camera/event catalogs and onboarding/subscription configuration. In 1.1.0, the app’s Remote Config fetch is gated by enabled usage analytics and foreground activity, normally with an hour between fetches; opening catalogs can trigger a due refresh, and foreground update notifications can refresh supported controls. Cached or bundled catalogs remain available when collection is disabled or the network fails. Firebase initialization and previously cached settings are distinct from optional interaction-event collection. Existing experiment assignments can remain locally after analytics is disabled. See Firebase privacy information.

Earlier 1.0.17 builds. Fetching Remote Config and saving a variant could occur independently of the optional analytics choice. Sending our interaction and experiment-exposure events required “Usage analytics”. These earlier configuration rules remain relevant to installed builds until they are updated.

Operational rollback settings (updated builds). The app checks a small, public JSON file hosted on GitHub Pages when it starts or returns to the foreground, with at least an hour between attempts. This is the same operational configuration for everyone: it lets us return onboarding and subscription-screen experiments to the usual behaviour. The request does not contain your location, measurements, analytics choice, account or installation identifiers, or app-interaction events. It does disclose your IP address and ordinary HTTP metadata to the hosting provider. GitHub states that Pages visitor IP addresses are logged and stored for security. These requests operate independently of optional analytics; they are not a periodic background refresh. This channel does not grant permission for analytics or personalise advertisements. See GitHub Pages data collection and the GitHub Privacy Statement.

Initial appearance configuration (updated builds). The app also reads a small public JSON configuration from GitHub Raw at startup. On a new installation it can set the initial light, dark or system appearance before the first screen; if it is unavailable, the built-in initial appearance is light. Your saved appearance choice is kept. This is uniform functional configuration, not an assignment to an analytics experiment or evidence of App Store campaign attribution. There is at most one request per app process, without background polling or a retry caused by analytics consent. The request contains no location, measurements, account, installation or advertising identifier, analytics choice or interaction events. GitHub receives your IP address and ordinary HTTP metadata. This request operates independently of optional analytics. See the GitHub Privacy Statement.

Subscriptions. Adapty processes subscription and transaction information, profile or installation identifiers, paywall interactions and technical device data to validate purchases, restore access and analyse subscription performance. In 1.0.17 and later, optional paywall-view logging requires “Usage analytics”. Purchase validation, restoration and the service's internal transaction records continue without that choice. The app disables Adapty IDFA/GAID and IP-address collection options and external analytics integrations; these settings do not disable all internal subscription processing. Apple handles payment details; we do not receive card numbers. See Adapty End-Users Privacy Policy.

Advertising. Google AdMob may process IP-derived approximate location, device identifiers, ad interactions and diagnostics to serve and measure ads and prevent fraud. Personalisation depends on your choices and applicable requirements. Where required, Google UMP presents advertising privacy choices; requests are gated on its permission to request ads. If you decline personalisation, limited or non-personalised ads may still involve data processing, or ads may be unavailable. Tracking permission is requested through Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt. Plus removes ads placed by ThermoWeather; independent third-party camera players may still show their own ads. Subscription processing continues; optional analytics and diagnostics follow your separate choices in 1.0.17 and later. Advertising choices and ATT do not grant permission for these optional streams. See Google Privacy Policy and AdMob data disclosures.

Diagnostic test builds. Builds explicitly configured for QA can offer a local, user-started diagnostic journal independently of optional Firebase collection. It records bounded technical actions, timing, errors, scenario/appearance and a random local run identifier, not microphone audio, readings, notes, city names, coordinates, API keys or raw request bodies. It is not enabled in ordinary production builds. An exported report is shared only through an explicit user action; if you send it to support, we process it as support correspondence. QA analytics is labelled as test traffic and separated from production reporting.

4. Legal bases (GDPR)

The legal basis depends on the processing and applicable law. Requested forecasts, subscription validation and other necessary service functions may rely on performance of a contract (GDPR Article 6(1)(b)); required records may rely on legal obligations (Article 6(1)(c)). Service reliability and product improvement may rely on legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)) only where applicable law permits and those interests are not overridden by your rights. You can object to that processing and disable analytics or crash reporting independently without losing Plus or core features. Advertising, tracking and any analytics or device-storage processing that requires prior consent remain subject to that requirement (Article 6(1)(a), where applicable). A default-enabled switch, continued app use or this notice is not a declaration of user consent and does not replace legally required consent.

Versions 1.0.17–1.0.19 used separate opt-in choices for usage analytics and crash reports. This revision accurately describes the 1.1.0 defaults; it does not retroactively change earlier choices or legal bases. Advertising consent and ATT are separate from analytics/diagnostic settings.

Earlier versions. In 1.0.16 and earlier, analytics services initialised at startup and release builds enabled crash reporting without separate in-app switches. The earlier notice described crash/usage statistics under legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f)). The new choices do not apply retroactively; you can contact us about earlier processing using the rights described in section 6.

5. Retention and deletion

On your device. Direct acoustic sessions retain their recordings and session/reference metadata until you delete the session or the app; they are not subject to the ordinary 500-reading limit. The iOS session directory is excluded from operating-system backup. Exported copies are separate. Legacy echo samples are discarded after analysis. Manual body-temperature values last only for the current session. Ambient history keeps up to the most recent 500 readings. Local preferences, saved places, calibration data, notification settings, accepted catalogs and caches can remain between launches. Deleting the app removes its local data; offloading can preserve it, and other system backups follow your Apple settings.

Acoustic exports. If you explicitly export an acoustic session, a ZIP can contain the original WAV recordings, reference readings, device/audio details and session/calibration metadata, including a reference-sensor identifier if present. Background speech can be audible in the recording. Review what you share. Nothing is sent to the developer automatically; if you choose to send a report to support, we receive and use it to investigate your request. In-app session deletion cannot recall copies held by a recipient. Temporary export files can remain in the device cache until removed by the app or operating system.

Exports and diagnostic files. Share images are temporary local cache files. When a later image export is prepared, inactive files older than 24 hours are eligible for cleanup; files still in use by the share operation are retained until it completes. This is not a guarantee of deletion exactly 24 hours after creation. QA journals keep up to three reports, with reports older than 24 hours cleaned up when diagnostics next initializes. The operating system may clear cache files earlier. Copies saved or sent to another app, clipboard, backup or recipient are outside this local cleanup.

Analytics and configuration. Identifiable event and user data are subject to the Google Analytics property's retention settings; expiration and any reset on new activity follow those settings. Aggregated reporting can be retained separately. See Google Analytics retention controls. Remote Config installation identifiers remain until deletion is requested; Firebase describes removal from live and backup systems within 180 days after that request.

Operational configuration. The app keeps the accepted rollback flags, revision and last attempt time locally between launches. A network failure does not discard an accepted rollback; a newer valid configuration can change it. GitHub's security logs follow its retention policy; we do not promise their immediate deletion when the app is uninstalled or analytics is turned off.

Crash reports. Firebase states that crash traces and associated identifiers are retained for 90 days before removal from live and backup systems begins. This is not a promise that all backup copies disappear on day 90.

Subscription records. Records are retained as needed to validate and restore entitlements, resolve billing disputes, prevent fraud and meet legal obligations. Adapty retention follows our service terms and applicable law; eligible data can be deleted on our instruction. Deleting app data does not cancel your Apple subscription or erase purchase records that Apple must retain.

Advertising and provider logs. Google advertising data and weather/map request logs follow the providers' retention policies, including service operation, security and legal needs. See Google retention information and the WeatherAPI, MapMap, OpenStreetMap and official-source policies linked above. We do not control independent provider logs or promise their immediate removal when the app is uninstalled.

Support correspondence. We retain messages for handling your request and any related follow-up, dispute or legal obligation. We delete or anonymise identifiable information when it is no longer needed for those purposes.

6. Your choices and rights

Advertising choices can be reopened in app Settings → “Privacy settings” when the provider requires that option. Tracking permission can be changed in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking. Location, microphone and Bluetooth permissions can be revoked in iOS Settings. You can choose a city manually without location permission.

Open Settings → Privacy → Your privacy choices to change “Usage analytics” and “Crash reports” independently. On new 1.1.0 installations both start enabled, with no separate analytics/crash-report onboarding request. Turning them off does not require a subscription and does not remove core features. Saved choices on existing installations are preserved.

Other controls. Notification permission, notification categories, advertising choices and iOS tracking permission are separate controls. Delete a saved acoustic session from its session controls to remove the local recording and metadata. You can remove a Home Screen widget and stop a timer or Live Activity. System and recipient settings govern data already exported or displayed outside the app.

Turning a choice off stops future optional sharing. Events that occur while usage analytics is off are not saved by the app for later replay. Withdrawal does not automatically erase information already received by providers; their retention rules in section 5 and deletion-request rights still apply. It does not cancel a subscription or stop core weather, map, configuration and purchase requests. Advertising choices and iOS permissions are managed separately.

You may object to processing or request access, correction, restriction, portability or deletion, where applicable, by emailing tgstartup@icloud.com. Withdrawal does not affect processing that was lawful before withdrawal.

We respond within one month, subject to lawful extensions, and explain any request we cannot fulfil. We may need limited information to locate an app installation or purchase; do not send passwords, card details or health readings. We do not require you to create an account to submit a request. Where possible, we instruct our processors to delete identified records and explain any legal retention exceptions. Uninstalling alone does not submit such a request. You may also complain to your local data protection authority.

We require third parties receiving personal data through our app to protect it at least as strongly as this policy and Apple's applicable privacy requirements. Providers acting on our behalf must limit processing to authorised purposes and apply appropriate confidentiality and security measures. We remain responsible for selecting and configuring the services used by our app; linking a provider policy does not waive that responsibility.

Services may process data outside your country. Where required, international transfers must use recognised safeguards, such as an adequacy decision or standard contractual clauses. Provider policies describe their locations and safeguards. Connections to the listed services use HTTPS; no method of storage or transmission can be guaranteed completely secure.

These legal pages and the operational rollback JSON used by updated builds are hosted on GitHub Pages; initial appearance configuration is hosted on GitHub Raw. Opening a page or requesting either JSON sends a web request to GitHub, which processes technical information such as your IP address to operate and secure its service. See the GitHub General Privacy Statement. We do not add analytics, advertising, cookies or third-party scripts to these pages. Copies viewed on Google Drive are served by Google under its privacy policy. No account or app installation is required to read these web pages.

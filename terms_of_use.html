1. The service

ThermoWeather shows third-party weather forecasts and brings together temperature and humidity values from compatible Bluetooth sensors, Wi-Fi sensors connected through Home Assistant, and manual entries. The acoustic method and household-signs guide provide experimental or approximate room-air estimates. Supported features also include weather maps, air quality, ventilation and indoor or outdoor drying recommendations, measurement history, a lunar calendar, solar-day calculations and polar-aurora viewing conditions. Core features are available without a ThermoWeather account.

Home Assistant and authorized devices. Connect only sensors and servers you own or have permission to use. Wi-Fi sensor access requires a compatible Home Assistant server, authorized access, an HTTPS connection and suitable temperature and humidity entities. Use the Home Assistant or device-vendor terms for your server and connected equipment. If your server relies on a vendor service such as Smart Life or Tuya, that integration must work in Home Assistant for ThermoWeather to receive its readings.

Dictation and Siri. Voice input fills an editable manual reading from the values you dictate. Verify the values, temperature units and any room note before saving. In supported iOS versions, you can choose one sensor for a Siri shortcut that reports its last available reading and its time. The result can be outdated if the sensor or server has stopped reporting. Siri and speech recognition depend on Apple's availability, the device and language support. If Apple’s online recognition is needed, the app asks before sending audio for that dictation request. The Privacy Policy describes what is shared.

2. Accuracy and intended use. Forecasts are probabilistic third-party estimates. Room temperature should come from a compatible external sensor or a separate thermometer entered manually. The acoustic method is experimental and is not a calibrated thermometer; the household guide gives rough estimates only, and the iPhone heat indicator is not air temperature. The app is not a medical device and does not measure body temperature with iPhone sensors: body values are entered manually and do not replace a medical thermometer or professional advice. Do not rely on the app as the sole data source where measurement or forecast errors could cause harm to life, health or property.

Acoustic measurement. The main 1.1.0 flow uses the built-in speaker and supported microphones, without requiring a wall, and records a local measurement session. A legacy echo method, where available, is separate. Device support, audio routing, preparation, geometry, noise, humidity and calibration affect whether a valid result can be obtained. Accuracy on iPhone has not been established as a calibrated room thermometer; any displayed uncertainty is a model estimate, not a validated error bound. The flow may return recordings, quality results or an unsupported/invalid result rather than a temperature. Use an external thermometer for a reliable reference. Local audio storage and optional export are described in the Privacy Policy.

Ventilation, moisture and drying. Recommendations are estimates based on available measurements and forecasts. Indoor recommendations require appropriate data for the selected room or sensor; a weather forecast alone does not measure your room. A suggested drying window does not guarantee when laundry will dry, and ventilation guidance does not diagnose or remediate mold or replace building, health or safety advice. Recheck conditions before acting, especially for future intervals.

Maps, aurora and calendar. Weather layers show the model and time identified in the app, not necessarily observations. Aurora activity is probabilistic; actual visibility also depends on darkness, cloud cover, viewing position and local conditions. A future calendar date may have astronomical information without an available aurora map or weather forecast for that night. A map for the nearest time is not a forecast for every selected date. Lunar events and times are estimates for the selected place. Official warning feeds have their own coverage and update delays; check the responsible authority for urgent decisions.

Timers, local threshold alerts and Sky reminders. Delivery depends on notification permission, iOS scheduling and device operation. Temperature, humidity and sensor status alerts rely on the selected source and the data it reports. Outside-temperature thresholds are checked when the app receives fresh weather data. A lost signal can prevent a check. Sky reminders use the available event catalog or forecast data and follow supported reminder and quiet-hour settings. Check current conditions and official alerts before taking action. Timers and notifications provide information for you to act on.

Server notification pilots. The configured Home Assistant pilot can send a Wi-Fi threshold or sensor-status notification through Cloudflare and Firebase Cloud Messaging. Delivery requires an active and authorized test installation, a running agent and a reachable Home Assistant server. Pilots can stop or change during testing. Scheduled outdoor-temperature checks are still under development. Threshold notifications and weather warnings are informational, and they should not be your only source for urgent, health or property decisions.

Aurora history and seasonal planning. Kp is a planetary activity index, not a guaranteed local sighting probability. NOAA map percentages describe the model at its stated time and location, rather than a personal chance of seeing aurora. Historical observations and seasonal cloud/darkness/activity summaries can help compare places and months, but do not predict that an event will repeat. Long-range activity outlooks and weather forecasts have different coverage and uncertainty; missing dates are not filled by treating current conditions as future observations.

Astronomical events, guide and cameras. Solar-day calculations, event dates and local visibility are estimates based on the selected date and place. The Sky guide's constellation and comet diagrams are educational illustrations. Catalog corrections and viewing conditions can change what you see. Do not look directly at the Sun through unfiltered optics or without appropriate solar-viewing protection. Camera availability, image freshness, stream quality and operator schedules vary. Still images and panoramas are identified separately from continuous video. Sources may stop, change their player or display independent advertising. Plus does not guarantee uninterrupted access to third-party media.

5. Sharing results and acceptable use

Where available, sharing exports the selected supported result as an image or text after your action. Review its city, dates, units, source and freshness before sharing. A saved reading or forecast snapshot is not continuously updated after export. A drying/ventilation card does not start a timer or reminder for its recipient or transfer a Plus entitlement. Sharing a Plus-only recommendation requires access when the card is prepared and sent. The receiving app and any recipient handle exported copies under their own terms; deleting a local card cannot recall them.

Measurement history and CSV. In supported builds you can export the eligible readings in the current history selection. Review the time zone, units, source and whether each entry is a measurement or estimate before comparing or sharing it. CSV timestamps use UTC. A CSV is a copy of the selected data at export time. You are responsible for choosing the recipient or storage location when you use the system share sheet.

Acoustic session exports can contain raw audio, device/session metadata and reference readings. Export only recordings you are entitled to share. The app does not automatically upload these files.
